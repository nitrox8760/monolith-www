Visit report vs site certificate vs logbook

Paperwork

4 min read · Updated 23 August 2026

The three documents are easy to mix up after a test. One covers this visit. One covers the building. One is the running history. Using each for its job keeps the client’s file in order.

They are rarely explained side by side, which is how the wrong form gets left with the client, or the logbook never gets updated because the certificate looked like enough. Here is the split.

At a glance

Document What it is When you need it
Visit report What you did on this test After a monthly or annual visit — hand it over before you leave
Site certificate Standing record for the installation Annual; kept with the building file and logbook
Logbook Running history of tests and repairs Every test — monthly and annual

Most annual visits need the visit report and an updated site certificate, plus a logbook entry. A monthly flick test often only needs the logbook — unless the client wants a visit report every time.

Visit report (periodic inspection report)

This is the paperwork for this test. Client and building, what was checked, what failed, when you are next due, and who signed it off. Two pages in Beacon’s layout.

Use it after a monthly or annual test when you want something the client can file with the logbook — not just a scribbled note.

Site certificate (periodic inspection and testing)

This is the record for the building — client, premises, your declaration, and the inspection results table. Four pages. It sits with the emergency lighting logbook as evidence the installation has been periodically inspected.

It is not for brand-new installations. New work, alterations, or verifying an old system against a newer code edition need different certificates.

The logbook

The logbook is the running history: every monthly function test, every annual duration test, defects found, and remedial work. It should stay on the premises (or be available there) for the life of the system. What each entry needs: emergency lighting logbook — what to record.

Visit reports and site certificates support the logbook — they do not replace it. Gaps in monthly entries are a common finding even when the annual certificate looks fine.

For the testing schedule itself, see how often to test emergency lighting.

When you need which

  • Monthly function test — always update the logbook. Issue a visit report if the client wants one (many do for contractor visits).
  • Annual duration test — logbook entry, visit report for the work just done, and usually an updated or newly issued site certificate.
  • New installation — completion paperwork, not these periodic forms.

Rewriting the fitting list every year is how certificates and logbooks drift apart. Keep a living site register — the fitting list you update between visits instead of rewriting from scratch — so each visit starts from the same fittings.

Frequently asked questions

Is the visit report the same as the site certificate?

No. The visit report is for this test — what you checked and signed off. The site certificate is the record for the building as a whole, kept with the emergency lighting logbook.

Do I need both after every annual test?

Often yes. Leave a visit report for the work you just did, and update or issue the site certificate so the building’s standing record matches. Always update the logbook.

Is this for new installations?

The periodic visit report and site certificate are for existing installations. Brand-new work usually needs a completion certificate, not these periodic forms.
