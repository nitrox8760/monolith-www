How often to test emergency lighting

Testing

5 min read · Updated 23 August 2026

Two rhythms matter on site: a short monthly check that every fitting comes on, and a once-a-year run that proves the batteries last. Miss either, and the logbook has a gap an inspector will notice.

This is for people who test emergency lighting on site. It is practical guidance, not a substitute for BS 5266 or legal advice. The Fire Safety Order puts the duty on the building’s responsible person; your job is to test properly and leave a record they can show.

The two tests that matter

Under BS 5266 practice in the UK, emergency lighting is not a once-a-year job. You need a monthly function test (often called a flick test) and an annual full-duration test — typically three hours for commercial premises.

Every month

Function test

Brief flick test — confirm every fitting comes on. Keep it short so you do not drain the battery.

Every year

Duration test

Full rated run — usually three hours. Catches tired batteries the monthly test will miss.

Monthly function test

Prove every luminaire and illuminated exit sign comes on under battery. Keep it short so you do not drain the pack. Step-by-step: how to do a monthly emergency lighting test.

  • Simulate mains failure with the test key, inhibit switch, or central test facility.
  • Walk the route. Confirm each fitting lights and looks normal — not dim, flickering, or missing a head.
  • Restore supply. Note any failures by location or fitting ID before you leave the floor.
  • Record the date, who tested, and the outcome in the site logbook.

Trained building staff often do the monthly function test. You still need a clear register of fittings and a place to log defects — otherwise next month’s tester starts from scratch.

Annual duration test

Run the system on battery for its full rated duration. This is the test that catches tired batteries. Step-by-step: how to run an annual duration test.

  • Plan for the full rated period — usually three hours — and for recharge time afterwards.
  • Check fittings during the run, not only at the start. Failures at the two-hour mark are common.
  • Note which fittings failed, when, and what remedial work is needed.
  • Issue the visit paperwork and update the site certificate / logbook as the client expects.

See visit report vs certificate vs logbook if you are unsure which document to leave after the annual.

What to record

Inspectors care as much about the paper trail as the fittings. For each test, the logbook should show:

  • Date and type of test (monthly function or annual duration)
  • Who carried it out
  • Result per fitting — or a clear pass with failures listed by ID / location
  • Remedial action and when it was completed

What usually fails

  • Dead or weak batteries — especially on older fittings that still pass a short flick test but die mid-duration.
  • Missing fittings on the list — stores, plant rooms, and late additions never made it onto last year’s spreadsheet.
  • Gaps in the monthly log — the annual looks fine; the months in between are blank. That is still a compliance gap.
  • Rewriting the register every visit — if you start the fitting list from scratch, you will miss something. See keep a living site register (update the fitting list between visits instead of rewriting it).

Frequently asked questions

How often must emergency lighting be tested in the UK?

BS 5266 expects a brief monthly function test of every fitting and a full-duration discharge test once a year — usually three hours for commercial premises. Results should be recorded in the site logbook.

Can building staff do the monthly test?

Often yes, if they are trained and know the layout. The annual duration test is usually done by a competent contractor who can assess batteries, failures mid-test, and issue the paperwork.

What should I record after each test?

Date, type of test, who tested, pass/fail per fitting (or a clear summary with failures listed), and any remedial action. That record sits in the logbook and may sit alongside a visit report.
