Emergency lighting logbook: what to record in the UK

Logbook

4 min read · Updated 30 August 2026

The logbook is the running history of tests and repairs — not the visit report, not the site certificate, and not the fitting list. If months are blank, the annual looking tidy is not enough.

What the logbook is for

Inspectors and duty-holders need a trail: that monthly function tests happened, that the annual duration ran, and that failures were fixed. The logbook is that trail — continuous, dated, and attributable to a person.

It does not replace the site register (the list of fittings) or the site certificate (the standing record for the building). It sits alongside them.

What each entry needs

For every monthly or annual test, record at least:

  • Date of the test
  • Type — monthly function / flick, or annual duration (note the rated period)
  • Who carried it out
  • Result — clear pass, or failures listed by fitting ID / location (and time into a duration test if relevant)
  • Remedial action — what was done or arranged, and when it was completed / retested

How to run the tests themselves: monthly flick test · annual duration test.

Monthly gaps still matter

A common finding: the annual certificate is neat, and six months of the logbook are blank. That is still a problem. The rhythm is monthly function plus annual duration — missing months are missing evidence.

  • Do not invent past entries to “fill the book”.
  • Do fix the process so the next twelve months are tested and written up.
  • Building staff often do the monthly; contractors often do the annual. Both need a shared register and a place to write.

Logbook vs register vs certificate

Thing What it is Updates when
Logbook Running history of tests and repairs Every monthly and annual test
Site register List of fittings (IDs, locations, types) When fittings are added, removed, or changed
Site certificate Standing record for the installation Typically after the annual / periodic inspection

Where the logbook lives

On paper it is often a bound book in the fire cupboard or FM office. Digitally it may be a shared drive, a spreadsheet, or software that keeps history on the site. What matters is that the next tester can find it, add an entry, and that an inspector can follow the trail.

Where software helps

Frequently asked questions

Is an emergency lighting logbook a legal requirement in the UK?

The responsible person must be able to show that emergency lighting is properly maintained and tested. In practice that means a running record — paper or digital — of monthly and annual tests. This page is practical guidance, not legal advice.

Is the logbook the same as the site certificate?

No. The logbook is the running history of tests and repairs. The site certificate is the building’s standing record after periodic inspection and testing. You usually need both.

What if months are missing from the logbook?

That is a compliance gap even if the annual certificate looks fine. Fill forward properly from now — do not invent past entries. Fix the process so monthly tests are done and recorded.
