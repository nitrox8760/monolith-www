Not all emergency lighting software is the same

Different tools cover different jobs. Work out which category you need, then compare products inside it.

  • Purpose-built for emergency lighting tests — work through fittings monthly and annually, keep a living site register (the fitting list you update between visits instead of rewriting from scratch), and issue the visit report and site certificate from that visit. Beacon sits here.
  • Multi-discipline fire and security platforms — fire alarms, extinguishers, intruder, and emergency lighting certificates under one roof. Useful if you already cover several fire disciplines; FireCert is the main UK example. These platforms are strong on certificates across many systems; they are less focused on fitting-by-fitting emergency lighting tests on site.
  • General electrical certificate apps — EIC, EICR, PAT, and a stack of other forms; emergency lighting is one certificate type among many. Elec-Mate and Pro Certs sit here.
  • Business ops for service companies — recurring schedules, dispatch, invoicing, accounting sync, with compliance documents issued from the job. HiveSuite sits here.

Floorplan and facilities asset tools (Plotstuff and similar) are another category — useful for mapping devices across a building. They are not aimed at signed BS 5266 paperwork from an on-site emergency lighting test.

What to look for on site

If you spend most of your time on site testing luminaires, check products against this list:

  • Offline in plant rooms — no signal should not stop you finishing the job. You need to record results and sync later.
  • A living site register — fittings persist between visits; you update, you do not recreate. See keep a living site register.
  • Both test types on the same list — monthly function and annual duration, with defects on the fitting.
  • Paperwork from the visit — a visit report and periodic site certificate without typing the whole building into Word again.
  • Import — bring the spreadsheet or logbook you already have, so setup is not a weekend of typing everything in again.
  • Audit trail — who tested what, when; signed documents that stay as signed.

Software is most useful when you have more than one site, a rotating team, open retests that get lost on paper notes, or a logbook with gaps because last month’s paper record cannot be found.

Product by product

Beacon

Beacon is emergency lighting test software built for UK contractors who test buildings on site. Field runs on phone or tablet — including plant rooms with no signal. Console is the office view for sites, team, history, and branded PDFs.

What it does well

  • Living site register — fittings, rooms, test switches, and floors carry forward. Import the spreadsheet you already have instead of typing the whole site in again.
  • Monthly and annual on the same register — function tests and full-duration runs with a timer per test switch, defects and photos on the fitting until they are closed.
  • Paperwork from the visit — visit inspection report and periodic site certificate, each with its own reference, without rewriting the list in Word. Issued documents stay as signed.
  • Console for the firm — multi-site overview, invite technicians, company branding on the documents you leave with the client.
  • Free in open beta — no card to start.

What it is not

  • Not a fire-alarm / extinguisher / intruder platform — Beacon is emergency lighting only.
  • Not an EICR / BS 7671 certificate library.
  • Not a full job-scheduling and invoicing suite (Xero, quotes, recurring invoices).

If you need a fitting list that carries forward, monthly and annual on the same register, and paperwork that matches the visit — whether emergency lighting is all you do or a major part of a wider fire business — Beacon is built for that work.

FireCert

FireCert is a UK fire and security compliance platform. It is built around multi-discipline certificates — fire alarms, extinguishers, intruder, CCTV — with emergency lighting as one of the disciplines under that roof.

What it does well

  • BS 5266 certificate wizards (completion, periodic, existing-site compliance) with locked PDFs and QR verify.
  • Building-level compliance dashboards and automated due-date reminders across disciplines.
  • Client portal and a shared building record if you already use FireCert for fire and security work.

Where it is weaker for emergency lighting tests

  • Built around certificate workflows and compliance status, rather than on-site testing with offline plant rooms and an annual duration timer per test switch.
  • Less emphasis than Beacon on a living register you import from a spreadsheet, open defects with photos that stay on the fitting until closed, or issuing both a visit inspection report and a site periodic certificate from the same visit.
  • From £79/month + VAT for a full fire platform — pricing reflects the multi-discipline coverage.

If you already run fire alarms and other disciplines in FireCert, it can still be a good fit for that wider work. For fitting-by-fitting emergency lighting tests and the paperwork from that visit, Beacon is the more focused option.

Elec-Mate

Elec-Mate is a mobile-first platform for UK electricians: 70+ BS 7671 calculators, apprentice training, AI agents, business tools, and 19 certificate types. Emergency lighting is one of those certificates — not the main focus of the product.

What it does well

  • One subscription covering EIC, EICR, PAT, fire alarm, EV, heat pump, and an emergency lighting certificate among others.
  • Offline-capable PWA for certificate drafts and calculators.
  • Strong fit if you already use Elec-Mate for day-to-day electrical work and only need occasional emergency lighting paperwork.

What it is not

  • Not emergency lighting test software in the sense of a living fitting register, monthly function test, annual duration timer, and both documents from the same visit.
  • Emergency lighting sits in a certificate library next to many other forms — expect a certificate-form workflow rather than a dedicated emergency lighting product.

Pro Certs

Pro Certs is a long-standing electrical certification app for iPad, Android tablets, and Windows. It produces EICR, EIC, minor works, and — more recently — BS 5266-1 emergency lighting certificates (completion, periodic, site compliance). Offline with cloud sync.

What it does well

  • Trusted cert library for electricians who already generate EICRs on tablet.
  • Multiple BS 5266 certificate flavours; photos on observations; multi-user portal.

What it is not

  • Same category as Elec-Mate: certificate forms, rather than a living emergency lighting register and on-site testing product.

HiveSuite

HiveSuite is UK service-business software: recurring job calendars, HiveCrew dispatch, certificate builder from jobs, Xero / QuickBooks sync. Plans from £34.99/month inc VAT. It includes BS 5266 emergency lighting document presets.

What it does well

  • Keeping monthly and annual visits on the calendar across dozens of properties.
  • Invoicing and accounting sync so the office side of a testing business stays joined up.

What it is not

  • Not a fitting-by-fitting testing tool with a luminaire register and duration timer. It is useful for the scheduling and office side of the business; recording each fitting on site is a different job.

Spreadsheet and paper

A careful spreadsheet can hold a register. Blank PDFs can hold a visit report and site certificate. On larger workloads, copies can diverge, months can go missing, and the certificate can disagree with the logbook.

Free blanks if you stay on paper: visit report template and site certificate template.

Which path fits you

  • Emergency lighting is your main work — monthly and annual, multi-site, need both PDFs from the visit → start with Beacon (free in open beta). Same answer if emergency lighting is a major part of a wider fire business.
  • You need one fire platform for alarms, extinguishers, and intruder certificates → FireCert for that wider stack. You may still want a dedicated tool for on-site emergency lighting tests.
  • You want one electrician app for EICRs and occasional emergency lighting certificates → Elec-Mate or Pro Certs may be enough; they are certificate apps rather than dedicated emergency lighting test products.
  • Your main need is scheduling and invoicing across hundreds of properties → HiveSuite for the office side; you may still want a register tool for the on-site tests themselves.
  • One small premises, one tester, fittings barely change → paper and the free templates below.

When paper is still fine

Paper can be enough if:

  • One small premises, one tester, fittings barely change.
  • The logbook is filled every month without chasing anyone.
  • You are happy printing blank forms and filing them with the building.

Frequently asked questions

Is there free emergency lighting test software in the UK?

Beacon is free in open beta — no card to start. Blank visit report and site certificate PDFs are also free if you still work on paper.

Do I need software for emergency lighting tests?

Paper is fine for a single small site with one careful tester. Software helps when you have multiple sites, monthly and annual on the same register, retests, a team, or paperwork that must match what you tested on site.

Is Elec-Mate emergency lighting test software?

Elec-Mate is a broad UK electrician platform — BS 7671 calculators, training, and 19 certificate types. Emergency lighting is one of those certificate types, rather than a dedicated fitting-list and living site register product.

How is Beacon different from FireCert?

Beacon is purpose-built for emergency lighting: Field for offline monthly and annual tests with a duration timer, a living register you can import from a spreadsheet, open defects with photos on the fitting, and visit report plus site certificate from the same visit. FireCert is a multi-discipline fire and security platform — certificate wizards, compliance dashboards, alarms / extinguishers / intruder under one roof. If you need fitting-by-fitting emergency lighting tests on site, Beacon is the more focused option; FireCert suits firms that want one platform across several fire disciplines.

What should emergency lighting software do offline?

Plant rooms and basements often have no signal. You should be able to work through the fitting list, record results and defects, then sync when you are back online — without losing what you recorded on the visit.
