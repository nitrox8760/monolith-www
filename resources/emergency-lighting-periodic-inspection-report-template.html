What is an emergency lighting visit report?

It is the document you hand over after a monthly flick test or annual discharge. BS 5266 expects regular testing and a record of the results. This report covers the building, the outcome of this visit, any changes since last time, and your signature.

A site certificate covers the installation as a whole. The visit report is for this test. If you test monthly and leave a report each time, the client builds a clear file next to the logbook.

When do you need one?

  • Monthly function tests — a short flick test to confirm each fitting works
  • Annual duration tests — a full battery discharge, often switch by switch
  • After repairs — when fittings were fixed and you re-tested on a follow-up visit
  • Before you leave site — so the client has paperwork in hand

Keep the completed report with the emergency lighting logbook. If how the building is used changes, that needs a new fire risk assessment — this report doesn’t replace it.

How to fill it in

  1. Download the PDF and open it, or print it for site.
  2. Fill in the client and building from the logbook.
  3. Record whether this was a monthly or annual test, and the overall outcome.
  4. Note any changes, open faults, and when you are next due.
  5. Sign it and give it to the person responsible for the building.

Visit report or site certificate?

Most contractors need both. The visit report is what you leave after a test. The site certificate is the record for the building — kept with the logbook, often alongside a year of visit reports.

Mistakes that undermine a visit report

A blank PDF only works if you fill every part. These are the gaps clients notice first.

  • Leaving fields blank

    Empty client, outcome, or next-due fields stand out. Finish every section before you leave site.

  • Handing over the wrong document

    A site certificate is not a visit report. Use this form for this test; keep the certificate for the building.

  • No signature

    A report without a dated signature isn’t a valid handover. A blank template won’t remind you.

  • It doesn’t match the logbook

    If the dates and outcomes don’t match what’s written in the logbook, the client can’t reconcile the file. Check before you leave.

Frequently asked questions

When do I need a visit report?

After each monthly or annual test. It records what you checked, the outcome, and who signed it off — the paperwork the client keeps with the logbook.

Is this the same as the site certificate?

No. The visit report is for this test. The site certificate is the record for the building. See the certificate template or visit report vs certificate vs logbook for that document.

How many pages is it?

Two pages: client and building, this visit’s outcome, your signature, plus changes and notes for the client.
